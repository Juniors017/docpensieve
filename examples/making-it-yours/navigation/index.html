Navigation

Module progress66%

Lesson 2 of 3.

A site of ten pages needs no navigation: the menu shows everything and the reader sees where they are. At forty, the same menu asks them to scroll past what does not concern them.

This lesson turns on three things, in the order you will want them.

1. Fold the menu

foldedSidebar: true,

The categories fold, and the branch of the page being read opens on its own — the reader lands where they are, not at the top of a list. A category that is also a page keeps its page as its first entry: the handle of a fold cannot be a link, so it would otherwise become unreachable.

Note

Nothing folds on a phone that was not folding already: on a narrow screen the whole menu folds above the content, whatever this setting says.

2. Put your own links in the header

headerLinks: [
  { label: 'Blog', href: '/blog/' },
  { label: 'Repository', href: 'https://example.com/repo' },
],

A target starts from the root of a version — /blog/ — or is a full address. A relative target is refused, because it would mean something different on every page.

An entry carrying columns opens a panel instead of leading anywhere:

headerLinks: [
  {
    label: 'Learn',
    columns: [
      {
        title: 'Start here',
        items: [
          { label: 'Install', href: '/guide/install/' },
          { label: 'First page', href: '/guide/first-page/' },
        ],
      },
      {
        title: 'Go further',
        items: [{ label: 'Components', href: '/components/' }],
      },
    ],
  },
],

Both, with different links

The panel and the documentation menu are declared separately and say different things: the header carries what a visitor looks for first, the menu carries the pages in their order. A site can have one, the other, or both.

href and columns together are refused — an entry either leads somewhere or opens a panel.

3. A row of links inside a page

Some pages need their own map: a landing page, the opening of a module. Menu places one where the page needs it, and it is the only navigation that lives in your text rather than in the configuration.

ColoursNavigationCallouts

<Menu label="This module">
  <MenuLink href="/examples/making-it-yours/colours/">Colours</MenuLink>
  <MenuLink href="/examples/making-it-yours/navigation/">Navigation</MenuLink>
</Menu>

Past a handful of entries, MenuGroup puts them under a title — the reader stops reading a flat list and starts reading two short ones:

On a narrow screen the row folds behind its label — the same fold as the header, and no more script than it.

On a phone

The header
Versions, your links and the search field move into a hamburger.
The menu
Folded above the content, closed, so the page starts at the page.
A page menu
The row becomes a single button, opening in place.

Try it the honest way: narrow your browser window until the layout changes. Every fold here is a native details element — it works with JavaScript turned off, and the keyboard opens it like any other.

Check yourself

Your panel entry has both a href and columns. What happens?

The build stops and says so. An entry that both leads somewhere and opens a panel would have to choose at the moment of the click, and the reader would never know which they get.

Which of the three needs the reader to load a script?

None.
